TRACE, a prototype for authorizing AI work in a course

These silent videos follow one quiz from course materials to a released grade and a student's challenge of it. They show how a course-grounded workspace can represent three decisions that a single "approve" step would collapse.

  • 4 videos
  • 28 minutes in total
  • No sound
  • Demonstration data: all names and records are synthetic
  1. 1 · Source

    What may AI use?

    Available course information becomes permitted evidence for one task.

  2. 2 · Artifact

    What does the course adopt?

    A provisional AI output becomes an official version someone stands behind.

  3. 3 · Consequence

    What may affect a student?

    Adopted work is released or applied, and can be withdrawn and reconsidered.

Source authorization

Before anything is generated, the instructor decides what the AI may build a quiz from, and says what the files cannot.

Why it matters

The excluded chapter is real course material that a retrieval system could use. Leaving it out is a teaching judgment that exists nowhere in the files.

Authorizing a source records permission. It does not prove the choice was right.

Video 2 · 7:02

Artifact authorization

An AI-generated assessment stays a draft until the instructor accepts each question. Acceptance attaches to a version, not to the assessment.

Select a step below to jump to it.

Why it matters

Generating work and accepting it are different actions. If AI later touches an approved question, the earlier approval does not carry over.

Approval records who stood behind a version. It does not certify that the version is correct.

Video 3b · 9:56

Consequence authorization

AI proposes a grade within seconds, but the student gets nothing until the instructor releases it. The student can then ask for the evidence to be reconsidered.

Select a step below to jump to it.

Why it matters

The student supplied classroom context the marking guide never held, and the system let that change the outcome.

Here the request returns to the instructor who approved the grade. That is a limit of this prototype, not a solution to recourse.

What this does not show

  • TRACE has not been evaluated. These videos show functionality, not evidence that it improves grading, fairness, or recourse.
  • Reconsideration goes back to the approving instructor. There is no independent route.
  • Release does not verify that a review took place, so approval could become routine.
  • Grading uses the submission, answer key, and rubric. There is no per-grade record of evidence, and students cannot see the authorization history.
